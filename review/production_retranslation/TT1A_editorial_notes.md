# TT1A production retranslation — editorial review

**Status:** Review-only. Nothing in this directory is consumed by the ROM build.

The purpose of this pass is to establish the English we would choose with no byte, line-width, dictionary, or renderer pressure. Technical adaptation comes only after editorial approval.

## Standard

- Exact Japanese/workbook semantics remain authoritative.
- Preserve the project's established character/register decisions.
- Use idiomatic contemporary American English unless the speaker/register calls for something else.
- Preserve Japanese cultural specificity when it is part of the joke or characterization (for example the Giants and consommé/miso questions).
- "Uncensored" means no sanitizing or euphemizing source material; it does not mean adding profanity or sexual content absent from the Japanese.
- Prefer complete sentences and intentional fragments over compression-driven telegraphese.
- Review copy uses proper typographic punctuation. Game-safe punctuation will be derived later.

## Material changes

| TT1A/g0/r0 | `It began that day...` | `It all began that day…` | Restores the force of `すべては` ("everything/all of it") and reads like deliberate opening narration. |
| TT1A/g0/r1 | `News: Dr. Simon, a reclusive genius in physics, issued this comment...` | `Newscaster: Late last night, Dr. Simon—the physicist known as a reclusive genius—made a remarkable statement about time travel.` | Turns a compressed information dump into broadcast English while retaining every fact and the "reclusive genius" epithet. |
| TT1A/g0/r2 | `years of research have brought time travel from dreams closer to reality` | `after many years of research, I believe I've brought what was once only a fantasy—time travel—much closer to reality…` | Gives Simon his established precise, formal voice and preserves the Japanese contrast between fantasy and realization. |
| TT1A/g0/r3 | `The Fortune Center presents "Today's Luck"` | `The Fortune Service Center presents "Today's Fortune."` | `運勢` is more naturally "fortune" here, and the full service-center name makes the television/service voice sound intentional rather than abbreviated. |
| TT1A/g0/r4 | `Your birth month?` | `What month were you born?` | Natural questionnaire English instead of a noun-phrase prompt. |

| TT1A/g0/r13 | `Have you cried alone hugging your knees?` | `Have you ever sat alone, hugging your knees, and cried?` | Natural English syntax while preserving the specific physical image. |
| TT1A/g0/r14 | `Would you put free time before work?` | `Would you rather not sacrifice your free time for work?` | The Japanese says the speaker does not want to work *at the cost of* leisure time; the old line could imply a broader work ethic judgment. |
| TT1A/g0/r15 | `Are you brand-conscious?` | `Do you care about brand names?` | More idiomatic American English and less marketing jargon. |
| TT1A/g0/r16 | `Do you put off what can wait?` | `Do you put off until tomorrow what you could do today?` | Preserves the specific today/tomorrow construction instead of flattening it into generic procrastination. |
| TT1A/g0/r17 | `Is self-protection up to you?` | `Do you believe only you can protect yourself?` | Restores `結局自分だけだ`—"in the end, only you yourself"—and removes an awkward abstraction. |
| TT1A/g0/r18 | `Do you hope to help society?` | `Do you want to contribute to society someday?` | Preserves the explicit future-oriented desire (`将来...たい`) documented by the authenticity pass. |
| TT1A/g0/r19 | `Do you want a short, full life?` | `Would you rather live a short but full life?` | Naturalizes the idiom `太く短く生きたい` without importing the stronger English cliché "live fast, die young." |
| TT1A/g0/r20 | `Is love enough?` | `Is love all you need?` | Directly captures `愛があれば何もいらない` rather than reducing it to a vague question about sufficiency. |
| TT1A/g0/r23 | fragmented personality-result prose | full connected paragraph | The Japanese is polished diagnostic prose. The old text reads like notes taken from it; the new version restores cohesion while preserving the social-but-anti-organization, isolation, and self-analysis traits. |
| TT1A/g0/r24 | `Hardworking, principled / Stubborn scholar type...` | full connected paragraph | Restores relationships between traits: caution produces few failures but ordinariness; diligence/principle/stubbornness form one scholar profile; consideration for others causes exhaustion; romanticism is inward despite awkwardness. |
| TT1A/g0/r25 | `Then come odd blunders. / Quick, curious insight fits you for reporting.` | full connected paragraph | `ぽか` is a careless blunder caused by abruptly losing motivation; curiosity plus sharp flashes of insight make the subject suited to journalism. The new prose expresses the causal structure. |
| TT1A/g0/r26 | `Money is another story. / Can be willful, selfish` | full connected paragraph | Restores the specific contrast: honor matters, money sense is weak, and strong self-assertion can turn selfish. Simon-like formality is not appropriate here; this is polished fortune-service prose. |
| TT1A/g0/r27 | `Keep a cool head and hold firm. Luck follows... Seal it with this line` | `Cool judgment and a strong will will guide you toward good fortune... Here's the line that'll win her over!` | Restores the explicit causal language and the comic dating-advice punch of `とどめのせりふ` rather than the unnatural "seal it." |
| TT1A/g0/r31 | `Whatever... Let's go!` | `Whatever… No time like the present!` | `善は急げ` is an idiom meaning to act promptly when doing something worthwhile; "Let's go" loses the expression entirely. |
| TT1A/g1/r0 | `Your smile makes the sun rise.` | `When you smile, it's as though the sun rises.` | Closer to `～ようだ`: a simile, not a literal causal claim. The deliberately corny pickup line remains corny. |
| TT1A/g1/r1 | `Earth's future is grim.` | `Earth's future is full of uncertainty.` | `不安だらけ` is literally full of anxiety/uncertainty; "grim" overstates certainty of disaster. |
| TT1A/g1/r2 | `people adrift today turn to fortune-telling... Oddly enough, it works.` | `modern people who feel lost have begun turning to fortune-telling for salvation... And strangely enough, the fortunes come true.` | Restores `救いを求める` (seek salvation/relief), the guide metaphor, and `当たる` (the predictions prove correct), while retaining the narrator's dry punch line. |

## Deliberate keeps

Some lines are already strong and should not be rewritten merely to create churn: `Do you prefer consommé to miso soup?`, `Please wait a moment.`, `Your personality is…`, the incantation itself, and several short protagonist reactions. The production pass is an edit, not a synonym generator.

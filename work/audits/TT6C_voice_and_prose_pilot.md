This pass continues the source-first voice/prose revision through the Nativity climax, the Devil's attempted possession and revenge, the Magi confrontation, the temptation flashback, the ruined 1995 timeline, the cross-chapter recap quiz, and the final exorcism/name reveal.

## Editorial direction

- Preserve exact Japanese meaning and control-code order.
- Keep the protagonist's internal voice contemporary, blunt, and emotionally immediate even when the surrounding material becomes religious or apocalyptic.
- Keep the Devil grandiose, sardonic, domineering, and selectively old-fashioned, reflecting his `washi`/authority role-language without turning him into a generic rustic speaker.
- Keep Caspar, Melchior, and Balthazar dignified and ceremonial, but avoid faux-King-James pastiche.
- Keep Joseph earnest and ordinary rather than scriptural; keep Mary quiet, perceptive, and firm when she recognizes that the false angel is the Devil.
- Let Jesus's speech be clear and scripturally framed without importing wording from a specific English Bible translation.
- Preserve source-explicit threats, insults, profanity, religious claims, possession, nuclear-war imagery, and the Devil's contempt without sanitizing or escalating them.
- Preserve recap-question facts exactly enough for the intended answers to remain recoverable.
- Preserve the source's intentional `Iesu` / English `yes` / Jesus name-reveal joke as far as natural English allows.
- Do not borrow wording from the removed external translation.

## Character/register decisions

### Protagonist

The protagonist remains a modern person even at the Nativity and during the final religious confrontation. His reactions therefore stay concise and contemporary. Elevated language is reserved for the explicit invocation of God's child/Jesus in the climax, not imposed on his ordinary thoughts.

### Devil

The Devil's `washi`, hostile second-person forms, grand declarations, and theatrical threats support a pompous, sardonic antagonist voice. Some inversion or elevated diction is appropriate when it remains readable, but the English avoids making him sound like a generic medieval narrator or a rural old man. Explicit contempt and profanity are retained when present.

### Caspar, Melchior, and Balthazar

The Magi speak with dignity and composure. Their Japanese is notably ceremonial/polite in places, so the English is more formal than Joseph or the protagonist, but not faux-scriptural. Their exposition about foreseeing the Devil and presenting offerings remains clear under the display limit.

### Joseph and Mary

Joseph remains earnest, credulous, and ordinary. Mary is quieter and more perceptive; her decisive refusal to surrender the infant is kept direct. Neither is rewritten into Bible-pastiche English.

### Jesus

Jesus's temptation dialogue is source-faithful and scripturally framed, but the translation does not quote a specific published Bible wording. The important logical content -- bread alone versus God's word, service to God alone, refusal of worldly power, and the sealing command -- is preserved directly.

## Recap-quiz integrity

The final confrontation quizzes the player on earlier chapters. Those prompts were treated as gameplay-critical data rather than free prose. The revised English retains the answer-bearing distinctions for museum exhibits, museum builder, Pierre's occupation, the Baron's wife, the German escape organization, Cougar's destination after crossing the border, medicinal herb, underworld guard, Meyer's livestock shed, the river-crossing animals, Mary's gift to Joseph, birthplace scene, Devil-pact history, and the `God's child` question.

In particular, the underworld question now describes the guard **in the underworld** rather than `guarding Hades`, avoiding a person/place ambiguity that could distort the intended answer.

## Display-safety revision

Runtime/visual review in preceding chapters established a practical target of no more than 23 visible characters between control events because exact-24-cell segments can appear clipped or lose right-edge punctuation even when they satisfy the repository's nominal 24-tile validator.

The pre-pass TT6C translation contained 34 scenario records with at least one exact-24-cell segment. Every one of those boundary records was revised.

A committed-file re-read also caught several cases where the initial 23-cell compression had removed source information. Those were corrected before this audit was finalized; display safety is not treated as permission to drop revenge motive, profanity, theological contrast, or other meaningful source content.

## Validation

The final TT6C candidate contains 106 scenario records and changes 54 of them.

Structural/editorial validation found:

- exact control-event sequence preserved for every changed record;
- no control-delimited English segment longer than 23 visible characters anywhere in TT6C;
- maximum final segment width: 23 characters;
- all 34 prior exact-24 boundary records revised;
- no new character repertoire introduced relative to the prior TT6C English text;
- revised TT6C visible text is 114 characters shorter overall than the prior branch version.

These checks validate source fidelity, recap-question semantics, text structure, voice/register decisions, and the stricter display-width policy only. Raw-character savings provide useful headroom but are **not** proof of dictionary-compressed packed fit. The canonical compression/release build remains the required fit gate before release approval.

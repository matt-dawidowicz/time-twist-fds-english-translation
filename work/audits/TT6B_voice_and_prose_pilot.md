This pass continues the source-first voice/prose revision through the journey to Bethlehem: Kashim's exhaustion in the barren country, the camel and three-man truth/lie direction puzzle, the arrival at Bethlehem, the stable animals, the sheep's religious quiz, and the animal-comedy interactions around Mary going into labor.

## Editorial direction

- Preserve exact Japanese meaning and control-code order.
- Keep Kashim/the protagonist's internal voice contemporary, physical, and comic without turning the donkey material into children's-book narration.
- Keep Joseph direct and colloquial, with his concern for Mary distinct from his sometimes comically casual treatment of Kashim.
- Keep Mary concise, worried, and practical during the journey.
- Preserve puzzle-critical direction, role, negation, and quiz wording exactly enough for the original deductions and answers to remain recoverable.
- Treat the camel's and cow's marked rustic forms as characterization rather than as evidence for a literal Japanese regional accent in ancient Judea. Use contractions, blunt syntax, and informal diction instead of phonetic eye-dialect.
- Keep the mare more self-important and status-conscious than the other animals.
- Keep the sheep's `-ja` role-language mildly elderly without faux-Biblical English.
- Preserve source-explicit profanity and insults without sanitizing them or inventing stronger material.
- Do not borrow wording from the removed external translation.

## Character/register decisions

### Kashim / protagonist

Kashim remains the contemporary protagonist mentally despite being a donkey. Physical misery, smell, hunger, brays, eating noises, attraction to the mare, and exasperation with the cow therefore use short modern reactions rather than storybook-animal diction.

### Joseph and Mary

Joseph is direct and practical. He can be caring toward Mary while being casually dismissive of Kashim's discomfort, which is part of the comedy. Mary remains more attentive to Kashim's condition and concise under physical strain.

### Camel

The camel has source-marked rustic speech (`dappe`, `ora`, informal endings). The English reflects this with loose, casual phrasing rather than spelling out a fabricated American rural accent. His explanation of honest/liar/unreliable roles stays especially precise because it defines the puzzle's terms.

### Cow

The cow is rougher and more heavily colloquial than the camel, with forms such as `wagannee`, `dabe`, and stretched filler phrases. The English uses `Dunno`, blunt syntax, profanity where the Japanese has it, and deliberately meandering filler without phonetic regional spelling.

### Mare

The mare is status-conscious, vain, and somewhat flirtatious/self-satisfied. Her objection is social familiarity and rank, not fear or hostility, and her Jerusalem haircut line retains the fashion joke.

### Sheep

The sheep's `-ja` speech marks an older-role voice. The English is concise and mildly old-fashioned in bearing, but avoids `thee`, `thou`, or scriptural pastiche. Quiz facts remain clearer than the voice effect.

### Innkeeper and travelers

The innkeeper is brusque but relents when Joseph explains the imminent birth. The three star-guided travelers remain simply travelers/men at this stage because the source does not yet require a more specific label.

## Puzzle and quiz integrity

The direction/truth puzzle was checked as a logical system after revision. The terminology is deliberately fixed to **honest**, **liar**, and **unreliable** across the camel's explanation and the three men's claims. With one of each role, the translated statements admit the same unique assignment as the source: right unreliable, middle liar, left honest; therefore west is the honest direction.

The sheep's religious quiz retains the answer-bearing propositions: Eve's temptation question, Judaism's sole God, Moses leading the Israelites out of a country, the Jewish king associated with Jerusalem's temple, and the Savior term meaning `anointed one`. No rewrite changes the information required to select the intended answers.

## Display-safety revision

Runtime/visual review in preceding chapters established a practical target of no more than 23 visible characters between control events because exact-24-cell segments can appear clipped or lose right-edge punctuation even when they satisfy the repository's nominal 24-tile validator.

The pre-pass TT6B translation contained 25 scenario records with at least one exact-24-cell segment. Every one of those boundary records was revised.

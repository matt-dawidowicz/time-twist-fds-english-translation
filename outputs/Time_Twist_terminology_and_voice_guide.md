# Time Twist terminology and character-voice guide

This guide accompanies the complete translation workbook. Exact Japanese always refers to decoded ROM text; reconstructed Japanese is editorial.

## Character voices

### Protagonist

- Japanese labels: おれ; occasionally ぼく inside speech
- First person: おれ (rough/plain masculine); ぼく when softening his tone
- Typical endings: plain modern speech; ぞ/な/だ; frequent stutters and exclamations
- Politeness: casual, with situational politeness
- Dialect/register: standard modern Japanese
- Verbal habits: internal commentary, comic panic, flirtation, abrupt disbelief
- Relationships: player viewpoint; protects the girl; pursues the Devil and Simon
- Recommended English voice: quick, contemporary, wry, and emotionally transparent; not macho parody

### Girl

- Japanese labels: おんなのこ
- First person: わたし
- Typical endings: feminine-coded わ/の and polite forms where appropriate
- Politeness: friendly to polite
- Dialect/register: standard Japanese
- Verbal habits: gentle teasing, surprise, growing confidence
- Relationships: modern woman tied to the museum incident and epilogue
- Recommended English voice: warm and poised, capable of teasing; avoid exaggerated femininity

### Devil

- Japanese labels: あくま
- First person: わし
- Typical endings: じゃ/のう/ぞ and grand declaratives
- Politeness: pompous and domineering
- Dialect/register: stock archaizing old-man/authority role-language, not a firm region
- Verbal habits: mockery, theatrical threats, inflated self-importance
- Relationships: possessor and antagonist across all historical chapters
- Recommended English voice: grandiose, sardonic, old-fashioned when useful; never generic rural dialect

### Dr. Simon

- Japanese labels: しもん; しもんはかせ
- First person: わたし
- Typical endings: formal explanatory prose
- Politeness: educated and controlled
- Dialect/register: standard Japanese
- Verbal habits: scientific explanation, ethical reflection, hesitation under pressure
- Relationships: physicist behind the Time Belt; recurring ally and target
- Recommended English voice: precise, formal, humane, occasionally halting

### Nagoya/Owari-coded businessman

- Japanese labels: おとこ
- First person: implicit
- Typical endings: がや/で; どえりゃー; うるしゃー; くりゃーて
- Politeness: loud, panicked, then boastful
- Dialect/register: clustered stylized Nagoya/Owari speech
- Verbal habits: money obsession and oversized resort plans
- Relationships: comic modern encounter near the museum
- Recommended English voice: brash regional-businessman flavor without mapping him to one real U.S. region

### Pierre and medieval commoners

- Japanese labels: ぴえーる; ちの; ごるどー; るごー
- First person: おれ
- Typical endings: rough contractions and emphatic particles
- Politeness: casual/rough
- Dialect/register: stylized commoner speech, not geographically Japanese in the fiction
- Verbal habits: gallows humor, fear, solidarity
- Relationships: townspeople and allies in 1428 France
- Recommended English voice: earthy medieval-adventure dialogue; readable, not faux-Shakespearean

### Bishop

- Japanese labels: しきょう
- First person: varies; often authority-coded
- Typical endings: archaizing じゃ/おれ commands under demonic influence
- Politeness: public authority masking cruelty
- Dialect/register: role-language rather than a regional diagnosis
- Verbal habits: decrees, threats, hypocritical religious language
- Relationships: antagonist in the Jeanne d'Arc chapter
- Recommended English voice: self-important ecclesiastical authority with theatrical menace

### Schmidt

- Japanese labels: しゅみっと
- First person: contextual
- Typical endings: terse declaratives
- Politeness: military restraint
- Dialect/register: standard Japanese
- Verbal habits: controlled speech and concealed resistance allegiance
- Relationships: apparent Nazi officer; ally to Cougar and Simon
- Recommended English voice: clipped and disciplined, softening only after his reveal

### Belle and George

- Japanese labels: べる; じょーじ
- First person: わたし / おれ
- Typical endings: rural and working-class stylization
- Politeness: deferential under coercion; intimate in private
- Dialect/register: source uses stylized nonstandard Japanese to mark class/setting
- Verbal habits: Belle is protective and devout; George is direct and frightened
- Relationships: mother and son on Meyer's farm
- Recommended English voice: plain rural speech with dignity; avoid minstrel caricature

### Joseph

- Japanese labels: よせふ
- First person: おれ
- Typical endings: casual masculine speech
- Politeness: familiar with Kashim; respectful to officials/elders
- Dialect/register: standard Japanese
- Verbal habits: worry, earnestness, occasional comic self-pity
- Relationships: Mary's betrothed; Kashim's owner
- Recommended English voice: earnest, worried, fundamentally kind

### Mary

- Japanese labels: まりあ
- First person: わたし
- Typical endings: soft feminine and polite forms
- Politeness: gentle and respectful
- Dialect/register: standard Japanese
- Verbal habits: calm concern and gratitude
- Relationships: Joseph's betrothed; mother of Jesus
- Recommended English voice: quiet, clear, and compassionate

### Village Elder

- Japanese labels: ちょうろう
- First person: わし
- Typical endings: じゃ/のう/おる
- Politeness: authoritative but caring
- Dialect/register: conventional elderly role-language
- Verbal habits: historical and prophetic exposition
- Relationships: leader in the Nazareth village
- Recommended English voice: measured elder voice; lightly old-fashioned, never hillbilly

## Control-code policy

Control tags are never translated. Their exact order is preserved in scenario records. A documented fixed-interface exception may retain the already validated playable control layout. The legend in the HTML workbook describes observed behavior without pretending each value has one universal linguistic meaning.
